# Journey Readiness Model Methodology

## Rationale: Explainable Multi-Criteria Scoring vs. Black-Box ML

The UdayamAI Journey Readiness Engine is deliberately built as an explainable, deterministic multi-criteria model based on the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) methodology, rather than a machine learning (ML) failure prediction model. 

This architectural decision is rooted in two critical realities of the Indian MSME landscape:
1. **Absence of Validated Datasets:** There is currently no generalized, validated, and publicly available dataset of business success/failure outcomes specific to first-generation and marginalized micro-entrepreneurs in India. Training an ML model on synthetic data or unrelated western corporate datasets would be scientifically indefensible and would introduce dangerous biases.
2. **Avoiding Harmful "Failure Probabilities":** Presenting a vulnerable, first-time applicant with a raw "% chance of failure" generated by an opaque black box can be profoundly discouraging and unethical, especially when the prediction cannot be empirically grounded. 

Instead, our model outputs a **Journey Readiness Score**, providing actionable, transparent feedback ("Here's what would help most") where every point can be traced back to a specific, named factor.

## Three-Layer Methodology

The Journey Readiness Score evaluates an applicant across three independently explainable layers:

### Layer 1: Entrepreneur Archetype Score (60% Weight)
This layer assesses the individual's foundational readiness using a 10-factor archetype model. 
- **Factors Assessed:** Industry experience, management experience, record-keeping habits, business planning, professional advice access, education, family business background, risk tolerance, self-confidence, and staffing plan adequacy.
- **Scoring Logic:** Each factor is scored on a 0-100 scale and multiplied by a specific weight. 
- **Weighting Rationale:** Financial factors (like record-keeping) and risk-taking propensity carry the highest weights. This is aligned with recent findings on Indian MSMEs (e.g., the 2025 DOAJ study, n=700), which identify capital management and risk tolerance as primary success drivers for marginalized entrepreneurs.

### Layer 2: Financial Readiness Score (40% Weight)
This layer evaluates the viability of the applicant's specific project proposal against scheme realities. 
- **Factors Assessed:** Own contribution adequacy, project cost realism, and eligible vs. recommended financing gaps.
- **Scoring Logic:** It heavily reuses the deterministic `FinancialCalculator` engine (PS 26091). It measures the "fulfillment ratio" (how much of the requested loan can actually be disbursed under scheme caps) and an EMI-burden proxy. If a project is severely out-of-range for available schemes, this score drops significantly.

### Layer 3: Regional Ecosystem Adjustment (±15% Bounded Multiplier)
The combined score of Layer 1 and Layer 2 is adjusted based on the structural realities of the applicant's geography.
- **Dimensions:** Entrepreneurial culture, finance access, human capital, innovation capacity, and institutional support proxies.
- **Scoring Logic:** An aggregate regional score modifies the baseline readiness by up to ±15%. A highly supportive ecosystem (like Maharashtra or Gujarat) provides a slight boost, while a challenging ecosystem demands higher individual resilience (slightly lowering the baseline score).

## Limitations and Calibration Path

**Explicit Limitation:** 
*The archetype weights in Layer 1 are literature-informed starting points, not empirically calibrated on Indian outcome data.* 

Because a validated historical dataset does not exist, the current weights rely on academic consensus (e.g., adapting Lussier's 1995 non-financial SME variables to the Indian context). 

**Calibration Path:**
Before production deployment, this model must undergo calibration via:
1. **Expert Pairwise Comparison:** Using formal AHP tools with domain experts (e.g., SIDBI officers, grassroots NGO workers) to refine the exact weighting ratios between factors like "Family Business Background" vs. "Record-Keeping Habits".
2. **Longitudinal Outcome Tracking:** Partnering with nodal agencies to track the 3-year survival rates of applicants scored by UdayamAI, gradually building the very empirical dataset that the field currently lacks.
